How many cycles does this engine have left?

Pick one of the 100 NASA test engines. Five regression models read its sensors and estimate the operating cycles remaining before failure. NASA recorded the true value, so you can see how close each model gets.

Where the sensors sit on the engine

A simplified section of a two-spool turbofan, drawn to show the engine parts the data comes from. The orange numbers are NASA sensor numbers.

Fan, LPC and HPC are the fan and the low- and high-pressure compressors. HPT and LPT are the high- and low-pressure turbines.

Where engine health matters in flight

The NASA data simulates large turbofans, the engines of the first three aircraft below. Estimating remaining life applies to every engine in a fleet, but a drone would need its own data and models.

The data behind it

Edit the sensor readings

Change any value, then estimate again. The models were trained on 18 inputs: the cycle count, two operating settings and 15 sensors. They learn remaining life capped at 125 cycles, so an estimate near 125 means “at least 125”.

How accurate are the models?

Official NASA test set, 100 engines. MAE and RMSE are errors in cycles, so lower is better. R² is higher-is-better.
